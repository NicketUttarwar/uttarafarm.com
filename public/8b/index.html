A season with Uttara Farm

This is a sample path, not a rigid script. Timing flexes with monsoon, soil results, and what each village circle needs most.

  1. Step 1

    Bhamb base

    Meet the circle, walk the plots, and agree which fields join turmeric or ginger for the coming season.

  2. Step 2

    Soil insight

    Test kits and advisors read the land. Nutrition plans follow the soil, not a one-size packet.

  3. Step 3

    Plant well

    Seed quality, spacing, and early care set the season. Field visits catch problems before they compound.

  4. Step 4

    Mid-season care

    Irrigation, disease watch, and nutrition adjustments. This is where discipline protects income.

  5. Step 5

    Harvest prep

    Grading and bundling start before the truck. Clean lots travel farther and sell fairer.

  6. Step 6

    Market & return

    Produce moves toward bigger buyers; learnings feed the next season and the cooperative structure.

Ask us

Something missing? Write to Vijay. We reply on WhatsApp when we can, usually within a few days.

Write to us
Do I need to leave other crops?

No. We start with turmeric and ginger as a high-yield focus. Many families keep other crops while they learn the new season rhythm.

Is this a loan scheme?

No. Uttara Farm is a social enterprise built around knowledge, tools, and markets. Income should come from better crops and fairer sales—not debt.

Who pays for soil kits?

We help organise access and shared use so costs stay practical for village circles. Details depend on the season and the local group.

Can young people join?

Yes. Reverse migration is part of the aim: skilled people earning a decent life in the village instead of only in the city.

What if the monsoon fails us?

We plan with advisors and adjust. Farming always carries weather risk; our job is to reduce the avoidable mistakes around it.

How do we leave the programme?

Participation is voluntary. Talk with the local lead; there is no lock-in designed to trap a family on bad terms.
